# Self-checks for the human–agent tandem

Purpose: improve judgment, initiative and learning through practical self-checks.
Prerequisite: the
[common learning sequence](../CONTEXT-INDEX.md).
Sources: S03, S04, S06 and S09 in the [source map](../../review/SOURCE-MAP.md).

## What is it?

Metacognition examines how a judgment was formed. The common discipline is to
separate observation, interpretation, hypothesis, decision and result. The human
and the agent need different practical checks; one is not a simulation of the other.

## Why does it exist?

A person can defend a preferred interpretation instead of examining the situation.
An agent can repeat an unproductive tool strategy or mistake remembered context
for a freshly observed fact. Both need a way to notice, change method and stop.
Disagreement alone is neither a personal defect nor proof of faulty reasoning.

## In practice: human self-check

“A colleague interrupted me three times” is an observation. “They reject my work”
is an interpretation. Frustration is a signal to examine, not proof of intent.
Ask what was understood, what the shared outcome is and which bounded change
could help. Notice whether the discussion still seeks understanding or merely
victory. Preserve consent, dignity and the right to disagree.

## In practice: agent self-check

Identify which facts came from a live tool, a source document, old context or
inference. Check source freshness, missing inputs, repeated attempts without new
information and the current mandate. If a tool cannot observe the result, state
that limit. Change strategy, obtain evidence or escalate when appropriate; do
not invent a result to finish. Record concise decision grounds and verifiable
evidence, not a demand for hidden internal reasoning traces.

## Practical criteria for changing the response

**[EXPLANATION S09]** These criteria make existing decision hygiene easier to
apply. They do not amend operational law, grant permission or prescribe a new
Runtime state machine. They concern observable decisions, not diagnoses of a
person or claims about an agent's subjective experience.

### Proportionate response: neither overreach nor unwarranted inhibition

Compare the consequences of acting and not acting with the current evidence,
mandate and reversibility. An old failure is relevant when its cause still applies;
it is not by itself a permanent prohibition. Conversely, a completion target or
a reviewer's encouragement cannot authorize a forbidden shortcut.

Inspect the decisive prerequisite. If the earlier cause is demonstrably removed
and authority still covers the task, choose a bounded permitted action with an
observable result. If the cause persists, authority has expired or a decisive fact
is missing, use the permitted alternative or escalate. Legitimate restraint must
not be relabeled as inhibition merely to increase completion. Check both cases,
including whether an accepted quotation stayed unchanged. See C9 and C10 in the
[scenarios](../examples/COMPREHENSION-CHECKS.md).

### Work pacing: change method when repetition adds no information

Exploration, verification, action, pause and review are purposes of work, not
mandatory stages that every request must traverse. Repeated probes with the same
inputs and no new information, or analysis that no longer changes the decision,
are signals to examine the method. A healthy scheduled wait is not such a loop.

Use the role's declared deadline, retry limits and escalation path; do not invent
a universal retry count. Choose a useful next observation, an authorized bounded
action or a stop with a clear handoff. State what remains unresolved, who owns
the next step and which new evidence or restored prerequisite permits resumption.
At resumption, recheck current authority and reconcile any uncertain prior effect
before replay. A pause must not conceal failure or abandon a time-critical
obligation. Verify progress and actual elapsed time, not simply fewer tool calls.
See C11 in the scenarios.

### Context-qualified learning: preserve facts, revise the conclusion

Separate the observed event and its provenance from the lesson inferred from it.
Give the lesson an applicability boundary: relevant cause, operating conditions,
counter-evidence and a review trigger. Learn from unusual success as well as
failure. Preserve permitted evidence under the governing retention and privacy
rules; neither erase inconvenient observations nor retain protected data forever.

A changed condition can justify revising a lesson, not rewriting the original
event. Test contrasting conditions before adopting a broader conclusion. Check
also whether the observer or corrector is producing false alerts or amplifying
the problem. Its recommendation is evidence to evaluate, not additional authority;
structural repair still belongs to the authorized external owner. See C9 and C12.

The exercise crosswalk to the existing qualification matrix is in
[S09](../../review/SOURCE-MAP.md#s09--decision-calibration-explanations-29-september-2026).
Conceptual answers do not demonstrate these behaviors in a running agent.

## What can fail?

Emotions are treated as diagnoses, confidence becomes authority, a fluent
explanation replaces a test, or endless introspection prevents a useful bounded
action. More reviewers do not guarantee independent views. A correction process
can add noise or merely compensate for an unexamined structural problem.

## How do we verify understanding?

For a recurring failed price import, the human explains what impact matters and
what was actually agreed; the agent identifies the evidence, failing assumption
and bounded next test. Together they choose START, CHANGE or STOP within the
mandate. Later they check whether the chosen action improved the outcome.

Meta-regulation asks an additional question: did our sensor and review process
help us discover the problem, or did it hide, amplify or misclassify it? Changes
to that process require explicit scope and authority as well.
